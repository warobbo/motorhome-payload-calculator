How to use

  1. Look up the UK registration if you can, then confirm MAM and axle ratings from the weight plate or VIN plate (driver’s door frame, under the bonnet, habitation door, or handbook).
  2. Enter Mass in Service from the V5, or better, a weighbridge ticket for the empty van.
  3. Add the people travelling today, current tank levels, gas and the kit on board.
  4. Watch remaining payload. Green is comfortable, amber is tight, red means you need to leave something behind.
  5. Use “What if I empty the water?” to see the cheapest weight saving before a weighbridge.

Payload FAQ

What is GVW on a motorhome?

GVW (Gross Vehicle Weight) is the maximum weight your motorhome is legally allowed to be when fully loaded — including passengers, fuel, water, gas, and all your gear. It’s set by the manufacturer and shown on the vehicle’s VIN plate. Go over it and you’re driving illegally, even if the overload isn’t obvious.

How do I find my motorhome’s GVW?

Check the manufacturer’s VIN plate (usually in the driver’s door frame, engine bay, or chassis), your V5C logbook (look for “Revenue Weight”), or the original vehicle handbook. If in doubt, weigh the van at a public weighbridge.

What is a C1 licence and do I need one for my motorhome?

A C1 licence lets you drive vehicles between 3,500 kg and 7,500 kg. If you passed your car test before 1 January 1997, you likely already have C1 entitlement on your licence. If you passed after that date, your licence is capped at 3,500 kg and you’ll need to pass a separate C1 test to drive a heavier motorhome.

Can I drive a motorhome over 3,500 kg without a C1 licence?

No — not legally. If your licence only covers category B (up to 3,500 kg) and your motorhome’s MAM exceeds that, you must either downplate the vehicle to 3,500 kg (where possible) or take the C1 test before driving it.

What is motorhome payload and how do I calculate it?

Payload is how much weight you can safely add to your motorhome before hitting its MAM. Calculate it as: MAM minus Mass in Service (the empty-van figure on the UK V5; handbooks often call this MIRO). What’s left is your payload for passengers, water, gas, and belongings.

How much payload does my motorhome have?

It varies by model and can be surprisingly low — often 300–500 kg once passengers and habitation equipment are factored in. Check your V5 for Mass in Service and MAM, or use our calculator above to work it out instantly.

What does MAM mean on a motorhome?

MAM (Maximum Authorised Mass) is the maximum legal weight of your motorhome when fully loaded. It’s the same figure as GVW — manufacturers and official documents just use different terms for it.

What is the difference between MAM and MTPLM?

None, practically speaking — they’re the same value. MTPLM (Maximum Technically Permissible Laden Mass) is the EU/technical term; MAM is the term used in UK licensing law. Motorhome brochures often list MTPLM, while your licence category depends on MAM.

What is Mass in Service — and does it include fresh water?

Mass in Service is the empty-van figure on the UK V5. Handbooks often call the same idea MIRO (Mass in Running Order). It usually includes the driver (75 kg), oils, 90% fuel and standard tools. Fresh water is normally assumed empty on UK motorhomes, so a full tank is extra payload. Always check your V5 or handbook, because converters differ.

Why do so many 3.5-tonne campers run out of payload?

A typical conversion leaves only 300–500 kg after Mass in Service. Two adults, a full 90–120 L water tank, gas bottles, a leisure battery, an awning and two bikes can use that allowance before food, clothes or a dog are added. That is why 3.5 t vans feel tight and why a weighbridge visit is worthwhile.

Does the driver count towards payload?

Mass in Service from the V5 usually assumes a 75 kg driver. Enter your real weight — only the extra kilograms above 75 are added. Extra adults, children and pets are additional. If you enter a weighbridge empty weight of the van only, the full driver weight is added.

How should I treat fuel, gas and water in this calculator?

Water weighs 1 kg per litre and is usually empty in Mass in Service, so a full tank is extra. Diesel is about 0.84 kg per litre. Mass in Service already includes about 90% fuel, so this page only adds the top-up (about 8 kg on a 90 L tank at 100%), unless you are working from a weighed empty van. A 9 kg propane bottle weighs around 18–19 kg when full because of the steel cylinder.

Is it illegal to drive a motorhome over its MAM?

Yes. Exceeding MAM can mean a roadside prohibition, a fine, points, and invalid insurance if you are involved in a collision. Overloaded axles are a separate offence even when the total is under MAM. There is no “small margin” in law — stay under the plated figures.

How accurate is this calculator compared with a weighbridge?

This is an estimate. Mass in Service and handbook MIRO figures are often optimistic, converters vary, and household kit is easy to under-count. Use the calculator to see where weight is coming from, then confirm on a calibrated weighbridge — ideally per axle — before a trip. The printed results are not a legal document.

Can I look up my motorhome by registration?

Yes. A UK plate lookup fills make and year, plus the DVLA revenue weight (often the plated MAM). DVLA does not supply model or Mass in Service. Model stays blank unless MOT history gives an exact van-maker model — coachbuilts such as Hymer are not filled with Ducato. Pick the platform from the list, and enter Mass in Service from the V5 or a weighbridge ticket. You can also choose make, model and year to apply typical 3.5t figures. Always confirm weights on the VIN plate and handbook.

What about tyre pressure if I change away from the original tyres?

The sticker inside the driver’s door is for the original tyres. If you fitted a different size, use the Tyres tool with your front and rear axle weights and the new sidewall. Typical Fiat Ducato / Peugeot Boxer notes

  • The Sevel van (Ducato, Boxer, Relay) on a 3,500 kg chassis is the UK’s default 3.5 t camper platform.
  • A converted panel van often leaves 350–550 kg of payload. Coachbuilts and winterised vans can be tighter still.
  • Front axle ratings are commonly around 1,850–2,100 kg and the rear around 2,000–2,400 kg — overloaded rear axles from bikes, tanks and boot kit are a frequent MOT and roadside issue.
  • A 90 L diesel tank full is about 76 kg of fuel. Mass in Service already assumes most of that, so topping right up adds only a little; water tanks are the bigger swing.
  • Chassis uprates exist, but tyres, brakes, speed limiter and your licence (B vs C1) must match. This calculator does not change the plated MAM for you. For a different tyre size, use the Tyres tool with your axle weights.
