How to use

  1. Use this when your tyres are a different size to the ones that came on the van. The usual pressures are usually on a sticker inside the driver’s door — those match the original size.
  2. Enter the tyre specification from the sidewall (fields start empty; a faint example is shown in the box).
  3. Add front and rear axle weights in kg from a public weighbridge (best), the Payload calculator, or the VIN plate (plated figures are usually maximums, not today’s weight).
  4. Read cold front and cold rear in bar and PSI. If your size or load index isn’t covered, the tool refuses — no guessed pressure.
  5. See Sources we use for which manufacturer tables we cover today.

Sources we use

Pressures come from published manufacturer tables — not guesses.

  • Covered today: Continental / General (LT, C and camping CP sizes we list) and Michelin Agilis CrossClimate C and LT, plus Michelin CrossClimate Camping CP where we have a matching published camping row (rear never below 5.5 bar).

If your size, brand or load index is not listed, the tool refuses a number. We will not invent one.

Don’t go above the maximum air pressure marked on the wheel (bar or PSI) — that limit is pressure, not weight. If you can’t find it, ask a tyre fitter. Also check pressures cold, and weigh the axles when you can.

Tyres FAQ

Where do these motorhome tyre pressures come from?

From published manufacturer tables, not guesses. We use the table that matches the tyre you typed. See Sources we use for the brands and sizes covered today. If your size, brand or load index is not listed, the tool refuses a number.

Which brands do you have tables for?

Today: Continental / General, and Michelin (Agilis C and LT, plus CrossClimate Camping CP where we have a matching published camping row). If your size or brand is not listed, this page refuses a number. We will not invent one.

Motorhome tyre pressure — cold or hot?

These figures are cold only. Cold means the van has been parked for a few hours, not just off a motorway. Pressure rises as tyres warm up. Do not let air out of a hot tyre to “match” this page.

Why isn’t my door-sticker pressure right for new tyres?

The sticker inside the driver’s door is written for the original factory size. A different size needs a pressure from that tyre’s published table at your axle weights. Copying the old sticker can under- or over-inflate the new rubber.

What if the tyre cannot carry the axle weight?

This page shows a clear fail and will not suggest a pressure if the axle weight is over the last published step for that size. Fit a higher-capacity tyre, or lose weight. A fitter and the vehicle plate win.

What does CP mean on a motorhome tyre?

CP means camping / motorhome tyre. It is not the same as a plain C van tyre. On a rear axle with two tyres we never show less than 5.5 bar, even if the table step is lower. Front stays on the table. Four tyres on the rear do not use that 5.5 bar minimum.

Why does a CP rear often show 5.5 bar?

The table can look low on a modest rear axle weight. For a camping tyre with two tyres on the rear we show the higher of the table step and 5.5 bar. The note still prints the table figure so you can see both. A fitter still wins.

What does the load index 120/117 mean?

Those numbers on the sidewall are load indexes. Each one is a separate row in the table — we will not silently use a different number. The first figure is for two tyres on the axle. The second is only for dual wheels (four tyres).

Is this a substitute for a tyre fitter?

No. Planning estimate only. If the size is outside the tables on this page, it refuses to invent a number. Confirm with the tyre maker’s published chart and a fitter.

How does this sit with the payload calculator?

Use the payload calculator for MAM and kit. Use this page once you know (or estimate) axle weights and have fitted a different tyre.
