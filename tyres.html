How to use

  1. Copy the cold front and rear figures from the driver’s door or pillar sticker, or the handbook. Do not copy a number from a forum or from this page.
  2. Type them in bar or PSI — the other unit fills in. The spare is optional.
  3. Paste a sidewall code such as 215/70 R15C 109/107 Q to see size, C-rating, load index and speed rating.
  4. Optional: add an axle load (kg) and the load index to see whether the marking looks enough. Treat that as a rough check only.

Tyres FAQ

Cold vs hot pressure — which should I use?

Use the cold figure from the door or pillar sticker, or the handbook. Cold means the van has been parked for a few hours, not just off a motorway. Pressure rises as tyres warm up. Do not let air out of a hot tyre to “match” the sticker.

Why does changing away from the original tyres matter?

The sticker is written for the tyres the van was designed around. A different size, C-rating or load index can need a different pressure and may not carry the same load. Ask the new tyre’s maker or a fitter — this page will not invent a pressure for the new rubber.

What does a dual load index such as 109/107 mean?

The first number is for a single tyre on that corner (the usual UK motorhome layout: two tyres on the axle). The second is for dual wheels — two tyres on one side, four on the axle. Dual figures are lower per tyre because they share the load. Most campers only need the first number.

Where do the kilograms for a load index come from?

From the industry-standard load-index table used across ETRTO / ISO tyre markings (for example load index 109 = 1,030 kg). It is a public index-to-kg map, not a full commercial pressure chart and not an approval for your van. Real capacity also depends on pressure, speed and the maker’s table.

Is this a substitute for the sticker or handbook?

No. It is planning help: a notepad, a converter and a decoder. Copy pressures from the vehicle, and treat the optional axle check as a rough comparison only. Wrong pressure or load can be dangerous.

How does this sit with the payload calculator?

Payload is about the van’s legal weight. Tyres are about the rubber that has to carry that weight. Use the payload calculator for MAM and kit, then this page to keep sticker pressures and read a new sidewall.
