How to use

  1. Use this when your tyres are a different size to the ones that came on the van. The usual pressures are usually on a sticker inside the driver’s door — those match the original size.
  2. Enter the tyre specification from the sidewall (fields start empty; a faint example is shown in the box, such as 225/75R16C 118R).
  3. Add front and rear axle weights in kg from a public weighbridge (best), the Payload calculator, or plated axle figures if that’s all you have (plated figures are usually maximums, not today’s weight).
  4. Read cold front and cold rear in bar and PSI. If your size or load index isn’t covered, the tool refuses — no guessed pressure.
  5. See Sources we use — that box names the manufacturer tables we use.
Planning estimate only — cold pressures. Wrong pressure or an overloaded tyre can be dangerous. The tyre maker’s own chart and a fitter always win. This page will not invent a number outside the table.

Sources we use

Pressures come from published manufacturer tables — not guesses.

  • Covered today: Continental / General (listed LT, C and camping CP sizes) and Michelin (Agilis CrossClimate C and LT, plus CrossClimate Camping CP where we have a matching camping row — rear never below 5.5 bar).

If your size, brand or load index is not listed, the tool refuses a number. We will not invent one.

Don’t go above the maximum air pressure marked on the wheel (bar or PSI) — that limit is pressure, not weight. If you can’t find it, ask a tyre fitter. Also check pressures cold, and weigh the axles when you can.

Tyres FAQ

Where do these pressures come from?

From published manufacturer tables. See Sources we use. If your size, brand or load index is not listed, the tool refuses a number.

Cold or hot?

These figures are cold only — van parked for a few hours, not just off a motorway. Do not let air out of a hot tyre to match this page.

Why isn’t the sticker inside the driver’s door right for new tyres?

That sticker matches the original factory size. A different size needs a pressure from that tyre’s published table at your axle weights.

What if the tyre cannot carry the axle weight?

This page shows a fail and will not suggest a pressure if the axle weight is over the last published step. Fit a higher-capacity tyre, or lose weight.

What does CP mean?

CP is a camping / motorhome tyre — not a plain C van tyre. On a rear axle with two tyres we never show less than 5.5 bar, even if the table step is lower. Front stays on the table.

What does the load index 120/117 mean?

The first figure is for two tyres on the axle. The second is only for dual wheels (four tyres). We will not silently use a different number.

Is this a substitute for a tyre fitter?

No. Planning estimate only. Confirm with the tyre maker’s published chart and a fitter.
