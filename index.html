MAM vs MIRO vs payload

MAM / GVM / MTPLM

The legal maximum the motorhome may weigh on the road. It is on the VIN plate, usually 3,500 kg on UK Ducato and Boxer campers.

MIRO / kerb / running order

The converter’s ready-to-drive weight. Typically driver + 90% fuel. Fresh water is usually empty. Treat brochure MIRO as optimistic until you weigh.

Payload

Everything else: extra people, water, waste, gas, food, bikes, dog, aftermarket batteries and solar. Payload = MAM − MIRO, before real-world extras eat it.

Where to find your weight plate, or VIN plate

Your motorhome’s weight plate is usually found in one of these places:

  • Driver’s door frame — most common location
  • Under the bonnet, near the engine bay
  • Inside a habitation door, on the entry step frame
  • Vehicle handbook, listed as MAM, GVW, or MTPLM

Look for “Maximum Authorised Mass” or “Gross Vehicle Weight”. This is the number you need. Axle ratings are often on the same plate. A DVLA lookup can suggest the figure, but the plate on the van is what the law uses.

Typical Fiat Ducato / Peugeot Boxer notes

  • The Sevel van (Ducato, Boxer, Relay) on a 3,500 kg chassis is the UK’s default 3.5 t camper platform.
  • A converted panel van often leaves 350–550 kg of payload. Coachbuilts and winterised vans can be tighter still.
  • Front axle ratings are commonly around 1,850–2,100 kg and the rear around 2,000–2,400 kg — overloaded rear axles from bikes, tanks and boot kit are a frequent MOT and roadside issue.
  • A 90 L diesel tank full is about 76 kg of fuel. MIRO already assumes most of that, so topping right up adds only a little; water tanks are the bigger swing.
  • Chassis uprates exist, but tyres, brakes, speed limiter and your licence (B vs C1) must match. This calculator does not change the plated MAM for you.

Payload FAQ

What is MAM, GVM and MTPLM?

MAM (Maximum Authorised Mass), GVM (Gross Vehicle Mass) and MTPLM (Maximum Technically Permitted Laden Mass) are names for the same legal ceiling: the most the motorhome may weigh on the road, including people, fuel, water, gas and everything you have loaded. For most UK Ducato and Boxer campers this is 3,500 kg. Going over it is illegal.

What is MIRO — and does it include fresh water?

MIRO (Mass in Running Order) is the manufacturer’s ready-to-drive weight. Under current European rules it usually includes the driver (75 kg), oils, 90% fuel and standard tools. Fresh water is normally assumed empty on UK motorhomes, so a full tank is extra payload. Always check your handbook, because converters differ.

Why do so many 3.5-tonne Ducato and Boxer campers run out of payload?

A typical conversion leaves only 300–500 kg after MIRO. Two adults, a full 90–120 L water tank, gas bottles, a leisure battery, an awning and two bikes can use that allowance before food, clothes or a dog are added. That is why 3.5 t vans feel tight and why a weighbridge visit is worthwhile.

Does the driver count towards payload?

If you are using the manufacturer MIRO figure, the driver is already included (usually at 75 kg). Extra adults, children and pets are additional. If you enter an actual weighbridge empty weight of the van only, add the driver as well as passengers.

How should I treat fuel, gas and water in this calculator?

Water weighs 1 kg per litre. Diesel is about 0.84 kg per litre. A 9 kg propane bottle weighs around 18–19 kg when full because of the steel cylinder. MIRO already assumes about 90% fuel, so this calculator only adds the difference from that assumption unless you are working from a weighed empty van.

Is it illegal to drive a motorhome over its MAM?

Yes. Exceeding MAM can mean a roadside prohibition, a fine, points, and invalid insurance if you are involved in a collision. Overloaded axles are a separate offence even when the total is under MAM. There is no “small margin” in law — stay under the plated figures.

How accurate is this calculator compared with a weighbridge?

This is an estimate. MIRO figures are often optimistic, converters vary, and household kit is easy to under-count. Use the calculator to see where weight is coming from, then confirm on a calibrated weighbridge — ideally per axle — before a trip. The printed results are not a legal document.

Where do I find the weight plate or VIN plate on a motorhome?

Your motorhome’s weight plate is usually found in one of these places: the driver’s door frame (most common), under the bonnet near the engine bay, inside a habitation door on the entry step frame, or in the vehicle handbook listed as MAM, GVW or MTPLM. Look for “Maximum Authorised Mass” or “Gross Vehicle Weight”. This is the number you need.

Can I look up my motorhome by registration?

Yes. A UK plate lookup fills make, model and year, plus the DVLA revenue weight (often the plated MAM). Model is inferred for common van platforms unless MOT history is connected. You can also type make, model and year to apply typical 3.5t MAM and MIRO figures. Always confirm weights on the VIN plate and handbook.

What is a realistic payload on a typical UK 3.5t conversion?

Many Fiat Ducato and Peugeot Boxer conversions on a 3,500 kg chassis have 350–550 kg of payload. Premium or winterised vans can be lower. A chassis uprate (for example to 3,650 kg or 4,250 kg) only helps if the axles, brakes, tyres and driving licence also allow it.

How to use

  1. Look up the UK registration if you can, then confirm MAM and axle ratings from the weight plate or VIN plate (driver’s door frame, under the bonnet, habitation door, or handbook).
  2. Enter MIRO from the handbook, or better, a weighbridge ticket for the empty van.
  3. Add the people travelling today, current tank levels, gas and the kit on board.
  4. Watch remaining payload. Green is comfortable, amber is tight, red means you need to leave something behind.
  5. Use “What if I empty the water?” to see the cheapest weight saving before a weighbridge.
