Sources we use

Pressures come from published manufacturer tables — not guesses. Blanks stay blank.

  • GREEN (full curve): Continental / General Tyre Databook for listed LT, C and camping CP sizes on 15–18″, and Michelin Agilis CrossClimate C and LT. We do not have Michelin’s Camping CP load/pressure table — we will not copy another brand’s camping table and call it Michelin.
  • AMBER (max load @ max cold pressure only): General Grabber LT from the official AT2 spec pages and A/TX flyer / specs page, and BFGoodrich All-Terrain T/A KO2 LT from the KO2 product sheet — retrieved 12 Sep 2026. Not a full curve. Dual stays blank where the maker did not print one. We did not use BFG commercial truck databooks (11R22.5 and similar).
  • General Grabber AT2 (AMBER) — GT18 spec pages PDF, retrieved 12 Sep 2026.
  • General Grabber A/TX (AMBER) — GT19 product flyer PDF and the A/TX specs page, retrieved 12 Sep 2026.
  • BFGoodrich All-Terrain T/A KO2 (AMBER) — KO2 product information PDF, retrieved 12 Sep 2026.
  • Industry CP guidance: TyreSafe publishes camping / motorhome guidance (a rear single is often 5.5 bar). That is industry guidance, not a Michelin Camping databook.

Tyres FAQ

Where do these motorhome tyre pressures come from?

From published manufacturer tables, not guesses. GREEN is a full maker load×pressure curve. AMBER is only the published max load at max cold pressure. See Sources we use. If your size, brand or load index is not listed, the tool refuses a number.

Which brands do you have tables for?

GREEN full curves: Continental / General (listed LT, C and camping CP) and Michelin Agilis CrossClimate C and LT. AMBER max-only: General Grabber LT and BFGoodrich All-Terrain T/A KO2 LT from the spec sheets named under Sources. We do not have Michelin’s Camping CP table. If your size or brand is not listed, this page refuses a number.

Should I use this page for a same-size C or CP renewal?

No. If you are putting the same C or CP size back on, use the vehicle door sticker or handbook. This page is for LT tyres or a different wheel and tyre set to the originals.

Why don’t you use a US lb/PSI chart or a generic van-tyre formula?

Those do not match the published tables for the tyre you fitted. This page reads the manufacturer table for that size and load index. It will not invent a pressure from a US chart or a one-size-fits-all formula.

Motorhome tyre pressure — cold or hot?

Use the cold figures. Cold means the van has been parked for a few hours, not just off a motorway. Do not let air out of a hot tyre to match this page.

Why isn’t my door-sticker pressure right for new tyres?

The sticker inside the driver’s door matches the original factory size. Same-size C or CP renewals should stay on that sticker or the handbook. A different size or an LT set needs a figure from that tyre’s published table — and only if we have one. Tyre capacity still does not let you exceed the van’s axle, wheel or legal limits.

What if the tyre cannot carry the axle weight?

This page shows a fail and will not suggest a pressure if the axle weight is over the last published step. Fit a higher-capacity tyre, or lose weight.

What does CP mean on a motorhome tyre?

CP is a camping / motorhome tyre — not a plain C van tyre. On a Continental / General CP rear axle with two tyres we never show less than 5.5 bar, even if the table step is lower. Front stays on the table. Michelin Camping CP is refused until we have Michelin’s official table.

What does the load index 120/117 mean?

Those numbers on the sidewall are load indexes. The first figure is for two tyres on the axle. The second is only for dual wheels (four tyres). We will not silently use a different number.

Is this a substitute for a tyre fitter?

No. Planning estimate only. Confirm with the tyre maker’s published chart and a fitter. If the size is outside the tables on this page, it refuses to invent a number.

What if you don’t have my size?

The tool refuses a number. We’ve noted this size for research when we can. Use the form on this page for anything else from the sidewall. We only add sizes from manufacturer databooks — we will not invent a pressure or email you one.

How does this sit with the payload calculator?

Use the payload calculator for MAM and kit. Use this page for an LT set or when the wheels or tyres are different to the originals. Same-size C or CP renewals belong on the door sticker. For axle weights: a public weighbridge is best; the Payload calculator is an estimate; the VIN plate often shows axle maximums, not today’s weight. Do not use the V5 for front and rear axle weights.

About

This tyre pressure tool sits on the Motorhome Payload hub. It is for LT tyres and for wheel or tyre sets that are different to the originals. Same-size C or CP renewals should use the door sticker or handbook. Your figures stay on this device and are not filled in until you restore them. We only use published manufacturer tables for the brands and sizes listed under Sources we use — GREEN full curves, or AMBER max load at max pressure. If your size or load index isn’t covered, the tool refuses a number rather than guessing. We’ve noted this size for research when we can; use the form on this page for anything else from the sidewall. This is a guide — check pressures cold, don’t exceed the wheel’s maximum air pressure, and never treat tyre capacity as permission to exceed the vehicle axle, wheel or legal limits.

Privacy

No account and no advertising cookies. Numbers stay in this browser’s local storage and are not filled in until you restore them. Nothing you type is sent to the payload lookup server. If a size is refused, the size, load index and brand already typed may be posted automatically so we can research a manufacturer table — you’ll see “We’ve noted this size for research.” The form is for an optional extra note or email. We will not email a pressure. We don’t upload your tyres.
